# Design Direction — Interactive Portal Website

## Creative direction

Create a premium, cinematic technology interface built around a rotating mechanical portal.

The experience should combine the atmosphere of a sophisticated spacecraft navigation system with the clarity and restraint of a professionally designed product interface.

The result should feel engineered, intentional and credible. Avoid the visual language of a generic gaming HUD, an off-the-shelf sci-fi template or an AI-generated collection of glowing panels.

## Visual principles

- **Monochromatic:** Use black, charcoal, graphite, gunmetal, silver and restrained white.
- **Depth through materials:** Create dimension with layered geometry, controlled highlights, shadows, subtle gradients and restrained metallic textures.
- **Strong focal point:** The wheel should dominate the composition.
- **Clear hierarchy:** The destinations must remain immediately understandable.
- **Restraint:** Every glow, line, particle and animation should serve a purpose.
- **Precision:** Alignments, spacing, ring geometry and typography should feel deliberate.
- **Usability:** Visual drama must never make navigation difficult.

Do not introduce accent colours without client approval. Keep the interface strictly greyscale.

## Composition

The rotating wheel should be the central visual anchor. Give it enough space to read as a substantial mechanical structure rather than a small decorative icon.

Explore layered concentric rings, segmented mechanical details, radial markers and carefully controlled depth. The design may suggest a portal or navigation mechanism without copying a recognisable copyrighted design.

Keep supporting interface information secondary. Use only the labels, indicators and status details that help visitors understand the experience.

Avoid defaulting to:
- A conventional hero section with a large heading and three cards.
- Generic dashboard panels.
- Unrelated floating glassmorphism cards.
- Excessive borders and decorative UI boxes.
- Random particles, lens flares or glow effects.
- Dense technical text that has no functional purpose.

## Typography

Use a restrained pairing rather than applying a futuristic display font to every element.

Suggested starting point:
- **Space Grotesk:** Main interface typography, headings and navigation.
- **Orbitron:** Selective use for short technical labels, destination identifiers or small interface details.
- **Monospaced font:** Optional use for coordinates, system status or compact metadata.

Orbitron is a direction to evaluate, not a fixed requirement. Check legibility, character spacing and how it works at small sizes before adopting it.

Avoid excessive uppercase text and widely tracked labels that reduce readability.

## Wheel and navigation states

Design clear visual states for:

1. **Idle:** Slow, subtle rotation. The wheel should feel active but not distracting.
2. **Hover/focus:** The relevant destination becomes more legible or illuminated. The response should be restrained and immediate.
3. **Selected:** The chosen destination is unmistakable. Other interface elements may recede.
4. **Transition:** The wheel and surrounding environment respond as one coordinated sequence.
5. **Reduced motion:** Provide a simplified transition or direct navigation while preserving clear feedback.

The wheel should not rotate so quickly that selecting a destination becomes difficult. Ensure labels remain readable and do not become disorienting as the wheel moves.

## Hyperdrive transition

The transition should feel like a deliberate departure sequence, not a collection of unrelated effects.

Potential stages:
1. Confirm the selected destination.
2. Increase the wheel's rotational energy.
3. Intensify selected highlights and surrounding light.
4. Create a sense of radial acceleration or forward movement.
5. Briefly transition through a controlled starfield, streak or tunnel effect if appropriate.
6. Complete navigation cleanly.

Use effects sparingly. Prioritise timing, easing, scale, depth and coordinated movement over visual noise.

The transition must not trap the visitor. Provide sensible handling for repeated clicks, interrupted navigation, browser history and reduced-motion preferences.

## Reference material

Use the images and screenshots supplied in `references/` as visual guidance. References should communicate specific qualities such as:
- Mechanical wheel geometry.
- Material and lighting treatment.
- Interface hierarchy.
- Hyperdrive motion and sense of speed.

Do not use reference screenshots as finished page backgrounds or substitute them for a genuinely implemented interface. Do not copy a reference composition wholesale.

## Responsive direction

On smaller screens:
- Preserve the wheel as the primary experience.
- Simplify secondary mechanical detail where necessary.
- Keep destination labels readable and tappable.
- Prevent clipping and accidental horizontal scrolling.
- Adapt the transition to device performance.
- Ensure the interface remains usable without hover.

## Design quality bar

The finished work should feel like a bespoke, professionally art-directed interactive experience. Prefer a few excellent, coherent details over many decorative effects.
